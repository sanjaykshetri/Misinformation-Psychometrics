# Construct definition: misinformation vulnerability

Misinformation vulnerability is treated here as a multidimensional latent construct capturing how people respond to problematic information under uncertainty.

## Core dimensions

### 1. Veracity discernment
The ability to discriminate true claims from false claims, independent of general response bias. A person with high discernment is better able to separate signal from noise when evaluating claims.

### 2. Response bias
A tendency to endorse claims as true or false regardless of claim validity. This is conceptually distinct from accuracy: a person may be highly confident but systematically biased in favor of accepting information.

### 3. Confidence calibration
The correspondence between reported confidence and actual correctness. Well-calibrated individuals express confidence that tracks performance quality, while poorly calibrated individuals may be overconfident or underconfident.

### 4. Evidence evaluation
The ability to interpret, weigh, and judge the reliability or strength of corrective evidence. This dimension is distinct from initial verdict formation.

### 5. Belief updating
The ability to revise judgments appropriately after receiving new information. A high score reflects movement toward the evidence-supported conclusion without overreaction or underreaction.

### 6. Verification competence
The ability to choose or perform effective verification actions when checking claims. This captures procedural skill and strategy selection rather than simple recall or general knowledge.

### 7. Sharing restraint
The tendency to avoid disseminating uncertain or misleading content. This is not pure accuracy: a participant may be relatively accurate but still share too much when content is emotionally salient or highly congruent with prior beliefs.

### 8. General knowledge
Background knowledge relevant to evaluating claims. This can support accurate judgment but may also interact with exposure, topic familiarity, and source effects.

### 9. Contextual distortion susceptibility
The degree to which item features like emotionality, political valence, or congruence with identity beliefs influence judgment beyond the underlying claim quality. This is a nuisance or moderating factor in the construct definition.

## Distinguishing constructs from outcomes

The goal is not merely to predict whether someone says "true" or "false" on a single item. The construct should explain patterns over repeated judgments, evidence-use behavior, confidence reporting, and verification actions.

A useful psychometric model therefore distinguishes:

- latent ability,
- latent bias,
- contextual distortion,
- and observed response behavior.

## Working definition

For this project, misinformation vulnerability is defined as the pattern of latent tendencies that make a person more likely to fail to distinguish reliable from unreliable information, miscalibrate confidence, resist evidence correction, act poorly in verification contexts, or share content without adequate scrutiny.

This definition is intentionally broad and hierarchical. It deliberately separates construct-level theorizing from the narrower measurement design used in Study 0.
